\section{Methodology}

The framework is an offline, three-stage pipeline: (1)~face detection and
tracking with a recall post-pass, (2)~per-track face replacement by a
generative model, and (3)~a fail-closed composition of the output video.
Temporal consistency is obtained by fixing one synthetic identity per track
rather than by a separate stabilization network.

\subsection{Stage 1: detection, tracking and recall}

Faces are detected by SCRFD-10GF~\cite{scrfd} (InsightFace's \texttt{buffalo\_l}
pack, inference only, 640\,px input) and associated across frames by a
ByteTrack-style tracker: detections at or above 0.5 are matched first and may
start new tracks, while detections between 0.1 and 0.5 can only extend an
existing track, which recovers partially occluded or bowed faces without
creating spurious tracks. Because the pipeline is offline, a post-pass over the
whole video then places a box wherever a tracked face almost certainly is:
tracks are emitted from their first detection rather than after a
confirmation delay, gaps of up to 30 frames inside a track are filled by
linear interpolation, each track is extended by three frames at both ends, and
tracks with fewer than three detections are kept for hiding but receive no
synthetic identity. Every box records its origin (detector, interpolated,
dilated, unconfirmed); only detector boxes, which carry landmarks, reach the
generator.

\subsection{Stage 2: face replacement}

Three generator families are compared in their published form: CIAGAN~\cite{ciagan}
(a conditional GAN driven by landmarks and a one-hot identity code),
GANonymization~\cite{hellmann} (pix2pix re-synthesis from dense landmarks) and
BLANKET~\cite{blanket} in its released code (an SDXL inpainting model with two
ControlNets and a refiner that generates one synthetic identity per track,
followed by FaceFusion's \texttt{inswapper} face swap and GFPGAN enhancer in
every frame; the paper describes a Stable Diffusion~1.5 variant). Blur and mosaic of the detected box are the censorship baselines.
Each track receives a deterministic seed, so a student keeps one pseudonymous
face throughout the video.

We modify the replacement in four places, which differ in where they act: on
the estimate of who the person is (C2), on the swap (P2), on a GAN's identity
code (P3) and inside the diffusion sampler (P1). None of the mechanisms is new
in isolation; what we study is where in a hybrid diffusion-and-swap pipeline
an identity push survives to the final video.

\textbf{(C2) Track-level real identity.} For every track, the ArcFace
(\texttt{w600k\_r50}) embeddings of its frames are aggregated into one estimate
of the real person's identity, weighted by a quality score (detection
confidence, face size, head yaw) with outlier gating; it also selects the best
frames from which the synthetic identity is generated. This estimate is the
target that P1--P3 push away from. Quality-weighted template aggregation is
standard in video face recognition~\cite{nan,qan}, and Zhu et al.~\cite{zhu-bmsb}
average the ArcFace features of a single-subject video before perturbing them;
we apply it per track, in multi-person footage, as a push target.

\textbf{(P2) Swap-stage push.} The identity embedding that conditions the swap is
moved away from that estimate,
$\hat{e} = \mathrm{normalize}(e_{\mathrm{synth}} - \beta\, e_{\mathrm{real}})$,
in the same space before \texttt{inswapper}'s projection. It generalizes the mix
FaceFusion already applies and BLANKET saturates
($1.35\,e_{\mathrm{synth}} - 0.35\,e_{\mathrm{frame}}$, with the current frame's
real embedding), with a controllable strength and the track's identity as the
target. Negative identity conditioning is also how diffusion anonymizers steer
away from the source (a negated ArcFace embedding in NullFace~\cite{nullface},
$(1-d)\,Z$ with $d>1$ in~\cite{kung}, negative guidance
in~\cite{revpers}); P2 applies it to a frozen swapper, whose output is known to
leak the target's identity~\cite{bondalakunta}. The push costs one vector
operation per track.

\textbf{(P3) CIAGAN code optimization.} Per frame, a few gradient steps move
CIAGAN's identity code to reduce
$\max(0, \cos(\mathrm{ArcFace}(x), e_{\mathrm{real}}) - \tau)$. The code is a
softmax over the 1200 training identities of CIAGAN's released checkpoint, so it stays inside their convex
hull; its logits start at the track's seed identity with probability 0.9 (a
saturated softmax has no gradient) or at the previous frame's code, and a
penalty $\lambda\lVert p - \mathrm{onehot}\rVert^2$ keeps it near the seed's
pseudonym. ArcFace is made differentiable by converting the same
\texttt{w600k\_r50} network to PyTorch and aligning the output with a
differentiable warp.
% DIAG: conversion parity min cos 0.99986; through the warp, min 0.986 (cv2 uint8 rounding).

\textbf{(P1) Diffusion guidance.} During BLANKET's identity generation, the
predicted clean latent of each of the last base-model steps is pushed by the
gradient of the same hinge, computed through the VAE decoder and the
differentiable ArcFace, in the manner of MPGD~\cite{mpgd}, without
back-propagating through the denoising network. The refiner pass that follows
is unchanged.
% TODO: add the MPGD bib entry (He et al., ICLR 2024, "Manifold Preserving Guided Diffusion").

\textbf{Release policies.} Two policies decide which generated faces are shown at
all. \emph{Hide-low} never generates a face whose detection confidence is below
0.5 (it is hidden by the fail-safe instead), since weak detections are the
partially visible faces the swap handles worst. The \emph{privacy gate} embeds
every generated face and hides it when its ArcFace similarity to the real face it
replaces, in the same frame, is at or above the video's in-domain verification
threshold at a 1\% false-accept rate, i.e.\ when a verifier would still accept
the pair.

Two engineering changes made BLANKET usable on classroom footage. The swap uses
the face Stage~1 found (its box and five landmarks) instead of re-detecting it,
and identity images the swapper's detector rejects are re-examined with a
more permissive detector. Together they raise the share of faces BLANKET
generates from \result{e3b/pooled/original/anonymized} to
\result{e3b/pooled/phase1/anonymized} (Table~\ref{tab:e3-coverage}).

\textbf{Adaptive sampling.} A face need not be generated in every frame. For each
track, the 106 landmarks of the real face are compared, after similarity
alignment, with those of the last frame whose face was generated; the face is
generated again when their mean distance reaches a threshold (in inter-ocular
units) or after a maximum gap, and otherwise the composition reuses the last
generated face. Fixed sampling (one frame in $N$) is the simpler alternative.

\subsection{Stage 3: fail-closed composition}

The output video never shows a face the pipeline knows about. Generated faces
are pasted back pixel-exactly (only the pixels the generator changed, so a
neighbour's background cannot overwrite them); every other box---a generation
failure, a post-pass box, a rejected swap, a face withheld by a release
policy---is hidden by the same track's most recent generated face (if at most 10
frames old) or by a neutral gray ellipse. Blur and pixelation are not used as
the fallback, since both are reversible or attackable for small faces.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Data.} Detection is benchmarked on the WIDER FACE validation split with
the official protocol. The video experiments use three classroom recordings
(239, 244 and 355 frames) with 16, 21 and 22 tracks, respectively, under the
pipeline's Stage~1 (Table~\ref{tab:e1-videos} reports the detectors at their
E1 threshold of 0.3).

% real person is reproduced in this paper; they are not a consented dataset of
% children (limitation).

\textbf{Privacy.} Two recognizers are reported side by side: FaceNet
(InceptionResnetV1 trained on VGGFace2), a held-out evaluator never used to
build or steer the pipeline, and ArcFace (\texttt{w600k\_r50}), the space in
which P1--P3 push. Thresholds are calibrated in-domain, per video, at a 1\%
false-accept rate, with genuine pairs from the same track and impostor pairs
from tracks visible in the same frame. Re-identification is closed-set: each
face is compared with the mean real embedding of every track (its own frame
left out), and rank-1/rank-5 record whether its own track ranks first or in the
top five; chance rank-1 is one over the number of tracks, 0.045--0.063 here.
We also report the relative rank-1 reduction,
$1 - \mathrm{rank1}_{\mathrm{anon}} / \mathrm{rank1}_{\mathrm{real}}$ (the
suppression rate of~\cite{terhorst}; the Privacy Gain of the survey~\cite{meden}
is the absolute difference $\mathrm{rank1}_{\mathrm{real}} - \mathrm{rank1}_{\mathrm{anon}}$).
Each metric is computed in three views: generated faces only (the generator's
quality), all faces with passthrough counted as a leak (the method as
published), and the final video after the fail-safe (what a viewer sees). The
primary measure is FaceNet rank-1 on the final video.

\textbf{Utility.} Between each real face and its replacement: emotion agreement
(8 classes, HSEmotion, reported as Cohen's $\kappa$ because classroom faces are
mostly neutral and raw agreement rewards outputs that read as the dominant
class), expression error (106 landmarks after similarity alignment, in
inter-ocular units), head-pose error (mean absolute error of pitch, yaw and
roll), age error and gender agreement (InsightFace), and re-detection. Each is
read against its noise floor, the same metric between consecutive real frames
of a track (Table~\ref{tab:noise}).

\textbf{Statistics.} Confidence intervals (95\%) are percentile bootstraps that
resample whole tracks; arms that share faces are compared on the same faces,
with a track-level sign-flip test. Results are pooled over the three videos and
stratified by face size, absolute yaw and detection confidence.

\textbf{Criteria, fixed before running.} An arm improves privacy when FaceNet
rank-1 drops and the paired interval against its reference excludes zero; it
preserves utility when its utility metrics stay within the reference arm's
interval, read against the noise floor.

\subsection{Experiments}

\textbf{E0, instruments.} Recognizer validity per video (true-accept rate at the
calibrated threshold) and the utility noise floors.
\textbf{E1, detection.} SCRFD-10GF, SCRFD-34GF and YOLO-FaceV2-s on WIDER FACE
(AP, recall-first operating points, latency) and on the three videos (faces per
frame, tracks).
\textbf{E2, generators.} Each generator and baseline in its published form, on
the same Stage~1 detections, scored in the three views.

\textbf{E3, contribution.} Every arm runs BLANKET (CIAGAN for P3) on the same
detections and is compared with its reference on the same faces.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{E3a (C2).} Each aggregation mode (first frame, best-quality frame,
mean, quality-weighted mean, exponential moving average) is fitted on half of a
track's frames and scored by its cosine to the other half.
\item \emph{E3b (coverage).} BLANKET as published, with several seed frames per
identity, and with Stage~1's detection in the swap.
\item \emph{E3c (P2 strength).} $\beta \in \{0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.8, 1.2, 1.6\}$
against BLANKET's own mix (\emph{native}) and no mix at all (\emph{none}), with
the quality-weighted mean as target.
\item \emph{E3d (P2 target).} $\beta = 1.2$ with each C2 mode as the push target,
against the first frame.
\item \emph{E3e (policies).} Hide-low and the privacy gate on top of P2 at
$\beta = 1.2$.
\item \emph{E3f (P3).} CIAGAN with the code optimization (10 Adam steps, step
0.5 on the logits, $\lambda = 1$) at $\tau \in \{0, -0.2\}$, against plain CIAGAN.
The planned $\tau = 0.2$ was dropped after a pilot on 20 frames: CIAGAN's
outputs already sit below it.
\item \emph{E3g (P1).} Guidance on the last 3 of the base pass's 7 UNet steps
(step 0.1 on the unit-RMS gradient, $\tau = 0.2$), on top of P2 at
$\beta = 1.2$, against P2 alone. It ran after a pilot showed a guided step costs
0.2--0.5\,s and 2--5\,GB extra. % DIAG: bridge tools/p1_spike.py, 2026-10-05
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The published view and the viewer's view disagree.} In its released code,
which passes through the faces it cannot generate (the paper hides the whole
frame instead), BLANKET is the least private method: FaceNet ranks the right student first for
\result{e2/pooled/blanket/facenet.rank1} of all faces, because BLANKET generates
only \result{e2/pooled/blanket/anonymized} of them and passes the rest through.
With the fail-safe, those faces are hidden instead, and the final video's rank-1
drops to \result{e2/pooled/blanket/facenet_final.rank1}; GANonymization, which
generates \result{e2/pooled/ganonymization/anonymized} of its faces, drops from
\result{e2/pooled/ganonymization/facenet.rank1} to
\result{e2/pooled/ganonymization/facenet_final.rank1}. Measuring only the faces a
method chooses to replace, or only the faces it publishes, misstates its privacy.

\textbf{Censorship and the GANs are private but erase behavior.} Blur, CIAGAN and
mosaic reach final-video rank-1 of \result{e2/pooled/blur/facenet_final.rank1},
\result{e2/pooled/ciagan/facenet_final.rank1} and
\result{e2/pooled/mosaic/facenet_final.rank1}, close to chance, but their emotion
agreement is at chance as well ($\kappa \le \result{e2/pooled/ciagan/generated/emotion_kappa}$)
and their head-pose error is 14--17\textdegree.

\textbf{BLANKET keeps behavior best on every probe,} with expression error
\result{e2/pooled/blanket/generated/expression}, pose error
\result{e2/pooled/blanket/generated/pose_err}\textdegree{}, emotion
$\kappa = \result{e2/pooled/blanket/generated/emotion_kappa}$ and gender agreement
\result{e2/pooled/blanket/generated/gender_agree}, but its generated faces are
the most re-identifiable (rank-1 \result{e2/pooled/blanket/generated/facenet.rank1}),
and large faces almost always (Table~\ref{tab:e2-size}): a swap keeps the
target's face shape, and a large face shows more of it. No method approaches
the emotion noise floor ($\kappa = \result{e2/pooled/_noise_floor/generated/emotion_kappa}$);
BLANKET's synthetic identities tend to smile, which a neutral classroom does not.

\textbf{Cost.} BLANKET is ten times slower than CIAGAN and two to three orders of
magnitude slower than blur and mosaic (Table~\ref{tab:e2-cost}):
\result{e2/pooled/blanket/fps} frames per second against
\result{e2/pooled/ciagan/fps} for CIAGAN. The diffusion model runs once per
track (2--3.5\,min per identity with the published sequential CPU offload); the
per-face work dominates a long recording. Section~\ref{sec:cost} shows that most
of this figure comes from where BLANKET's main process ran, and what each
speed-up costs.

\textbf{C2: a track is a better witness than a frame.} Fitted on the first half
of a track and scored on its second half (at least five frames later), the
mean of its embeddings reaches cosine \result{e3a/pooled/mean.heldout-cos},
against \result{e3a/pooled/first.heldout-cos} for the first frame and
\result{e3a/pooled/best.heldout-cos} for the single best-quality frame
(Table~\ref{tab:e3-identity}). Our hand-set quality weights do not help
(\result{e3a/pooled/quality_mean.heldout-cos} for the weighted mean), even when
the held-out frames are themselves weighted by quality. As a push target, however, no mode is significantly better
than the first frame (Table~\ref{tab:e3-p2-target}): at the strengths that
matter, the push moves the embedding far enough that the remaining error of
the target does not change the ranking.

\textbf{Coverage: generating every face costs privacy, and buys behavior.} With
Stage~1's detection, BLANKET generates \result{e3b/pooled/phase1/anonymized} of
the faces instead of \result{e3b/pooled/original/anonymized}, and the final
video's rank-1 rises from \result{e3b/pooled/original/facenet_final.rank1} to
\result{e3b/pooled/phase1/facenet_final.rank1}: a hidden face is never
re-identified, a swapped one sometimes is. The swapped faces are what keep the
behavioral signal (emotion $\kappa$ \result{e3b/pooled/phase1/emotion_kappa}
against \result{e3b/pooled/original/emotion_kappa}), so the rest of E3 starts
from full coverage and asks how much of that privacy can be won back.

\textbf{P2: a monotonic, significant reduction.} Pushing the swap's identity away
from the track's real identity lowers FaceNet rank-1 on generated faces from
\result{e3c/pooled/native/facenet.rank1} with BLANKET's own mix to
\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.2/facenet.rank1} at $\beta = 1.2$
(\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.2/delta-facenet.rank1}~$\pm$~\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.2/delta-facenet.rank1.ci})
and \result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/facenet.rank1} at $\beta = 1.6$
(\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/delta-facenet.rank1}~$\pm$~\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/delta-facenet.rank1.ci});
every strength from $\beta = 0.35$ on excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:e3-p2},

(\result{e3c/pooled/none/delta-facenet.rank1}). Expression
(\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/expression} against \result{e3c/pooled/native/expression})
and head pose (\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/pose_err}\textdegree{} against
\result{e3c/pooled/native/pose_err}\textdegree) do not move; emotion agreement
falls from $\kappa = \result{e3c/pooled/native/emotion_kappa}$ to
\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/emotion_kappa}, within overlapping intervals.

\textbf{The guidance recognizer overstates the gain.} In ArcFace, the space in
which P2 pushes, rank-1 falls from \result{e3c/pooled/native/arcface.rank1} to
\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.2/arcface.rank1} and saturates there
(\result{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/arcface.rank1} at $\beta = 1.6$); the held-out FaceNet
sees about a third of that reduction. A method evaluated only with the
recognizer it optimizes against would report a large privacy gain that does
not transfer.

\textbf{Release policies add a further, smaller gain.} On top of P2 at
$\beta = 1.2$, final-video rank-1 falls from \result{e3e/pooled/p2/facenet_final.rank1}
to \result{e3e/pooled/hide-low/facenet_final.rank1} with hide-low and to
\result{e3e/pooled/gate/facenet_final.rank1} with the privacy gate, both excluding
zero (Table~\ref{tab:e3-policies}). The gate reaches the same rank-1 while
generating \result{e3e/pooled/gate/anonymized} of the faces, against
\result{e3e/pooled/hide-low/anonymized} for hide-low, and has the lowest
verification rate (\result{e3e/pooled/gate/facenet_final.verified}); the
utility of the faces it shows does not change.

\textbf{P1 pushes inside the sampler, and the push does not survive it.} With
guidance, FaceNet rank-1 is \result{e3g/pooled/P1-c-0.1-+-P2/facenet.rank1}
against \result{e3g/pooled/P2/facenet.rank1} for P2 alone
(\result{e3g/pooled/P1-c-0.1-+-P2/delta-facenet.rank1}~$\pm$~\result{e3g/pooled/P1-c-0.1-+-P2/delta-facenet.rank1.ci}),
an interval that includes zero, and ArcFace rank-1 is unchanged
(\result{e3g/pooled/P1-c-0.1-+-P2/arcface.rank1} against
\result{e3g/pooled/P2/arcface.rank1}). The logs locate the loss. During the three
guided steps, the ArcFace cosine between the predicted clean image and the real
identity falls from \result{e3g/pooled/guided-cos.p1-c0.1.first} to
\result{e3g/pooled/guided-cos.p1-c0.1.last} on average over the
\result{e3g/pooled/guided-cos.p1-c0.1.identities} generated identities, and ends
below $\tau$ for a share of \result{e3g/pooled/guided-cos.p1-c0.1.last-below-tau}.
In the identity image that leaves the identity model, after the unguided
refiner, it is \result{e3g/pooled/identity-cos.p1-c0.1} with guidance against
\result{e3g/pooled/identity-cos.plain} without (median paired difference
\result{e3g/pooled/identity-cos.p1-c0.1.paired-median}; lower with guidance for
a share of \result{e3g/pooled/identity-cos.p1-c0.1.lower-share} of
\result{e3g/pooled/identity-cos.p1-c0.1.tracks} tracks).
Most of the push is lost between the last guided step and the image that
leaves the model: the final update and decoding of the base pass, and the SDXL
refiner, an unguided image-to-image pass that re-noises the face and denoises
it again from the text prompt alone. (The two measurements differ in the
alignment that precedes ArcFace, so the size of the loss is approximate.) What
is left then reaches the swap only through the identity embedding, which P2
already moves away from the real one. Guiding where the identity is decided
last (the refiner, or the swap itself) rather than inside the base pass is the
open variant; on this pipeline P1 adds 0.2--0.5\,s and several GB per guided
step for no measurable gain, and the final pipeline does not use it.

\textbf{P3 has little to push and does not move.} Plain CIAGAN's faces are already
re-identified near chance (rank-1 \result{e3f/pooled/ciagan/facenet.rank1}), so
a successful push could gain at most a few hundredths. It did not gain those:
the change is \result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau-0.0/delta-facenet.rank1} at $\tau = 0$ and
\result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau--0.2/delta-facenet.rank1} at $\tau = -0.2$. The cause is
the optimization, not an inactive objective. The hinge was active for a share
of \result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau-0.0/push.active} of the faces at $\tau = 0$ and
\result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau--0.2/push.active} at $\tau = -0.2$, and the optimizer
ran \result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau--0.2/push.steps} steps on average, yet the ArcFace
cosine to the real identity fell only from \result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau-0.0/push.cos-start}
to \result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau-0.0/push.cos-end}, and the code kept
\result{e3f/pooled/P3-tau-0.0/push.p-seed} of its mass on the seed identity.
The pseudonym penalty and the softmax's flat gradient near its start hold the
code in place, and within the convex hull of CIAGAN's training identities the
output's identity barely changes. A stronger optimizer would trade the
pseudonym's stability for a gain bounded by chance level; we do not pursue it.

\section{Final pipeline}
\label{sec:final}

The configuration we propose, and whose numbers are measured end to end, is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Stage~1 as described: SCRFD-10GF at 0.1, the ByteTrack-style tracker and
the recall post-pass.
\item BLANKET with Stage~1's detection in the swap (full coverage), the track
identity (C2) as the push target, and the swap-stage push (P2) at $\beta = 1.2$,
with its main process on the GPU.
\item The privacy gate at the video's 1\% false-accept threshold.
\item Adaptive sampling: a track's face is regenerated when the real face has
changed (0.08 of the inter-ocular distance) or after six frames, and reused in
between.
\item Fail-closed composition.
\end{enumerate}
On the three videos, without sampling, it re-identifies
\result{e3e/pooled/gate/facenet_final.rank1} of the faces at rank~1 in the final
video and verifies \result{e3e/pooled/gate/facenet_final.verified}, with
expression error \result{e3e/pooled/gate/expression}, head-pose error
\result{e3e/pooled/gate/pose_err}\textdegree{} and emotion
$\kappa = \result{e3e/pooled/gate/emotion_kappa}$ on the generated faces. Scored
on the composed video itself (E5), the same pipeline reaches rank-1
\result{e5/pooled/ref/facenet_video.rank1}; the difference is the compressed
composite and the reused faces as actually shown, which the final-video view of
E3 scores by the generated crop they came from. With adaptive sampling it reaches
\result{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/facenet_video.rank1}, at
\result{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/class-hours} GPU hours per hour of class.

Against BLANKET's released code (\result{e2/pooled/blanket/facenet.rank1}) this
divides re-identification by more than two while every face carries a synthetic
identity or is hidden. It remains well above chance and above blur
(\result{e2/pooled/blur/facenet_final.rank1}), whose faces carry no behavior: the
pipeline occupies a different point of the trade-off, not a dominant one.
$\beta = 1.2$ is chosen over $1.6$ because the guidance recognizer saturates
there, emotion agreement is higher, and it is the strength the policies were
measured on. P1 and P3 are not part of it.

\textbf{A behavior-first variant.} Turning GFPGAN off moves the pipeline along the
trade-off rather than off it: emotion agreement on the shown faces rises to
$\kappa = \result{e5/pooled/enh-off/emotion_kappa_video}$, the best of any
method we measured, at the price of hiding
\result{e5/pooled/enh-off/hidden-share} of the faces (the gate rejects the
swaps that stay too close to the real face) and a higher verification rate
(\result{e5/pooled/enh-off/facenet_video.verified} against
\result{e5/pooled/ref/facenet_video.verified}). Which of the two to use depends
on whether a study needs every student's face visible or their expressions
faithful.

\section{Limitations}

The three videos are not a consented dataset of children, so the study measures
the method on classroom-like footage, not on its target population. Gaze is not
measured: no permissively licensed estimator was validated on faces this small.
Human-rated pedagogical analyzability, a re-trained (parrot) attacker and a
demographic fairness analysis are not performed; the attacker is naive, which
overestimates privacy~\cite{hanisch}. Several components are licensed for
non-commercial research only: InsightFace's models (including \texttt{inswapper}
and the ArcFace recognizer), GFPGAN's StyleGAN2 and DFDNet parts and its FFHQ
training data, and the AffectNet benchmark the emotion classifier is reported on.
Privacy is measured against two recognizers and does not constitute a formal
guarantee.
The diffusion-based pipeline is slow: even with adaptive sampling, one hour of
class takes about two GPU-days, which restricts it to offline processing of
selected recordings. Faces the detector never finds
are not hidden; their rate is estimated from annotated frames.
