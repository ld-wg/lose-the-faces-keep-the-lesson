\begin{abstract}

% Factual draft (2026-10-22), to be rewritten.
Classroom recordings expose students' and teachers' identities. We replace every
detected face with a synthetic one and measure, on the video a viewer sees, how
much identity is removed and how much behavior (expression, pose, emotion)
remains. The pipeline has three stages: SCRFD-10GF detection with tracking and a
recall post-pass; replacement with BLANKET (one SDXL synthetic identity per track
and an \texttt{inswapper} face swap per frame); and fail-closed composition,
which never shows a known face as real. Our main change pushes the swap's
identity embedding away from the track's estimated real identity (P2). On three
videos, held-out FaceNet rank-1 drops from \resulten{e3c/pooled/native/facenet.rank1}
to \resulten{e3c/pooled/b-1.6/facenet.rank1} with no expression or pose cost; with
a privacy gate the final video reaches \resulten{e3e/pooled/gate/facenet_final.rank1},
against \resulten{e2/pooled/blur/facenet_final.rank1} for blur, which erases
behavior. Guidance inside the diffusion sampler (P1) and CIAGAN identity-code
optimization (P3) have no measurable effect. Running the main process on the GPU
and adaptive frame sampling bring the cost to
\resulten{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/class-hours} GPU hours per hour of class. No method
keeps emotion near its noise floor.

\end{abstract}
