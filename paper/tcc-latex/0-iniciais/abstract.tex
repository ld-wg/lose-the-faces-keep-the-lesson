\begin{abstract}

Classroom recordings expose students' and teachers' identities. This work
investigates whether swapping every detected face for a synthetic identity
reduces automatic face re-identification while keeping behavioral signal
(expression, pose, emotion). The pipeline has three stages: SCRFD-10GF detection
with tracking; one synthetic identity per track, generated with Stable Diffusion
XL and swapped onto the face in every frame by \texttt{inswapper} with GFPGAN
restoration, as in BLANKET; and a composition that hides every detected face
without a valid replacement. The evaluation uses two short videos (22\,s, 41
tracks), one of them seen only after the pipeline was frozen. The released
BLANKET code swaps \resulten{derived/frontier/e2-blanket/share} of the faces and
leaves the rest real; the pipeline shows a synthetic face for
\resulten{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/video_shown} of them and hides the rest. On the
new video, FaceNet, a recognizer kept out of the optimization, re-identifies
\resulten{e5/demo4/adapt-0.08/facenet_video_shown.rank1} of the shown faces
(chance: \resulten{derived/demo4/chance-rank1}), and an attacker who decides once
per student is right for \resulten{e5/demo4/adapt-0.08/facenet_video_track.rank1}
of them; against blurring, that attacker gets
\resulten{e2/pooled/blur/facenet_track.rank1}. Compared on the same faces, the
pipeline leaks less than the released BLANKET
(\resulten{e3/matched/pooled/gate/covered/facenet.rank1} against
\resulten{e3/matched/pooled/original/covered/facenet.rank1}), thanks to a push of
the swap embedding away from the real identity and to hiding the faces that are
still recognizable. A mask that separates the face core from its context shows
where the identity remains: what the swap does not replace (hair, forehead, ears,
outline, background) re-identifies as well as the whole image. Pose and
expression are kept better than by blurring and by generative adversarial
networks; no method keeps emotion. The projected cost is
\resulten{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/class-hours} GPU hours per hour of class. The
result is partial face de-identification, not anonymization: when privacy is the
requirement, hiding the face is still the choice.

\end{abstract}
