\begin{abstract}

Classroom recordings expose students' and teachers' identities. This work
investigates whether swapping every detected face for a synthetic identity
reduces automatic face re-identification while keeping behavioral signal
(expression, pose, emotion). The pipeline has three stages: SCRFD-10GF detection
with tracking; one synthetic identity per track, generated with Stable Diffusion
XL and swapped onto the face in every frame by \texttt{inswapper} with GFPGAN
restoration, as in BLANKET; and a composition step that hides every detected face
that has no valid replacement. On two short videos (22\,s, 41 tracks), the
released BLANKET code swaps \resulten{derived/frontier/e2-blanket/share} of the
faces and leaves the rest real; the pipeline shows a synthetic face on
\resulten{derived/frontier/e5-ref/share} of the faces and hides the others.
Swapped faces remain recognizable: FaceNet, a recognizer not used in the
optimization, re-identifies \resulten{e3c/pooled/native/generated/facenet.rank1}
of them (chance: \resulten{derived/demo2/chance-rank1}). Pushing the swap's
identity embedding away from the track's real identity lowers this to
\resulten{e3c/pooled/b-1.2/generated/facenet.rank1}, a difference that is not
significant after correction for multiple comparisons; after also hiding the
faces still verified as the student, the final pipeline reaches
\resulten{derived/frontier/e5-ref/rank1} among the shown faces, and an attacker
who decides once per student is right for
\resulten{e5/pooled/ref/facenet_video_track.rank1} of them. Blurring reaches
\resulten{e2/pooled/blur/facenet_final.rank1}. The leak is in the face swap,
which keeps the shape of the original face, not in the synthetic identity; SimSwap in place of
\texttt{inswapper} does not leak less.
Expression and pose are kept better than by blurring and by generative
adversarial networks (expression error of \resulten{e3e/pooled/gate/expression}
inter-ocular units, about five times the noise floor); no method keeps emotion.
The projected cost is \resulten{e5/pooled/adapt-0.08/class-hours} to
\resulten{e5/pooled/ref/class-hours} GPU hours per hour of class. The result is
partial face de-identification, not anonymization: the face swap evaluated here
does not replace blurring when privacy is the requirement.

\end{abstract}
